# Skip Tracing 101 for Texas Litigators

You have a perfect case. The petition is drafted, the filing fee is paid, and the process server is ready to go. But there’s one problem: the defendant has vanished. 

Maybe they’ve moved and left no forwarding address. Maybe they’re "evading service" by hiding behind a PO Box or a relative’s house. In Texas practice, you can’t get your case moving until you’ve perfected service. 

This is where **Skip Tracing** comes in. As an expert paralegal, skip tracing is one of my favorite "detective" tasks. Here is what it actually looks like and why a simple Google search isn't enough.

### 1. Beyond the Public Record
Anyone can search a name on a public site like Whitepages. But for a professional skip trace, we go deep into specialized, non-public databases (like LexisNexis Accurint or TLOxp). These databases aggregate billions of records that the general public can't see:
- **Utility Records:** The most reliable way to find where someone is actually living *now*.
- **Asset Records:** Boat registrations, aircraft licenses, and real estate deeds.
- **Social Media Deep-Dives:** People often hide from the law but can't resist posting a photo of their new dog in their new backyard.

### 2. The "Cross-Reference" Strategy
Finding an address is easy; finding a *current, active* address is hard. We cross-reference historical data (where they lived 10 years ago) with recent hits (where they registered a car last month) to build a "Heat Map" of their likely location. 

### 3. Locating Witnesses vs. Defendants
- **Defendants:** Usually want to stay hidden. We look for employment records, professional licenses (like a Texas Barber or Real Estate license), and active lawsuits where they might be appearing.
- **Witnesses:** Often just moved and forgot to update their info. For witnesses, we also look for "Associates"—relatives or former neighbors who might have a phone number.

### 4. Perfecting Service in Texas
Once we have a solid lead, we coordinate with your process server. If they still can't get close, we provide the documentation needed for a **Rule 106 Motion for Substitute Service**. In Texas, the judge needs to see that you’ve made a "diligent effort" to find the defendant. Our skip trace report *is* that evidence of diligence.
