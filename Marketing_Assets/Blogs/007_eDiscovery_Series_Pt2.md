# eDiscovery for Solo & Small Firms (Pt. 2): Collection, Processing, and Review

In Pt. 1, we identified where the data lives. Now comes the part that scares many attorneys: actually getting that data into a format you can use in court. 

This is the "Collection and Processing" phase, and if you get it wrong, you could be facing a spoliation claim or a very expensive Motion to Compel. Here is the Access Paralegal guide to doing it right without breaking the bank.

### Phase 3: Collection (The "Gold Standard")
The biggest mistake you can make is "Self-Collection" by the client. If you tell your client to "just forward me the relevant emails," you have already failed. 
Why? Because forwarding an email **changes the metadata**. The "Sent Date" becomes today's date, and the "From" field becomes the client. 

**Defensible Collection** requires pulling the data in its native format (like a .PST or .MBOX file) using tools that don't alter the "Last Modified" dates. For a solo, this doesn't mean you need a $50,000 forensic kit. It means you need a partner who knows how to use legal-grade extraction tools to pull a "clean" copy.

### Phase 4: Processing
Once you have the data, it's usually a giant, unorganized mess. Processing is the "Digital Filter" that makes the data manageable.
- **De-Duplication:** Removing identical copies of the same email thread.
- **Filtering:** Using date ranges and keywords to remove obviously irrelevant data (like "What's for lunch?" emails).
- **OCR:** Making scanned PDFs searchable.

Our **Multitool** handles this processing locally, ensuring your client's sensitive data never touches a third-party server during the "filtering" stage.

### Phase 5: Review
Review is where the billable hours live, but it's also where the bottlenecks happen. Small firms often try to review documents in a standard Windows folder. This is a nightmare for organization.

You need a **Review Protocol**:
1. **Responsive:** Does this document matter to the case?
2. **Privileged:** Is this an attorney-client communication?
3. **Hot:** Is this a "smoking gun" document for trial?

By using a standardized review platform (or even a well-organized Master Case Tree), you can move through 500 documents in the time it used to take to review 50.
