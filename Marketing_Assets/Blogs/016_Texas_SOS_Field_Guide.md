# A Texas Attorney's Field Guide to Secretary of State Filings

For transactional and business attorneys, the Texas Secretary of State (SOS) is the gateway to almost everything you do. Whether you’re forming a new LLC, filing an assumed name certificate, or merging two corporations, you’re dealing with the SOS.

While the **SOSDirect** system has made things faster, it hasn't necessarily made them simpler. A single "Rejected" filing can delay a closing by days and frustrate your clients. After handling hundreds of filings, I’ve built a "Field Guide" to help Texas attorneys navigate the process with zero friction.

### 1. The Name Availability Trap
Before you file anything, you must verify name availability. But here is the trick: "Available" in the SOS database doesn't mean "Safe to use." 
The SOS only checks for *distinguishable* names. It doesn't check for trademark infringement or common-law name rights. We always recommend a multi-level search (SOS, USPTO, and a general Texas web search) before committing to a name.

### 2. The "Registered Agent" Protocol
Every entity in Texas must have a Registered Agent and a Registered Office. This cannot be a PO Box; it must be a physical street address. 
**Common Mistake:** Using the attorney's office as the registered agent without a clear agreement on who is responsible for tracking service of process. We help you set up a protocol for how these notices are handled once they arrive.

### 3. Purpose and Management Clauses
For LLCs, the "Certificate of Formation" (Form 205) is the DNA of the company. Solos often use the default "Any lawful purpose" language, but for specialized businesses (like professional entities), the "Purpose" clause must be more specific. Similarly, being clear about **Member-Managed** vs. **Manager-Managed** from day one prevents a mountain of corporate governance headaches later.

### 4. Assumed Names (DBAs)
In Texas, if an entity does business under a name other than its legal name, it must file an **Assumed Name Certificate**. 
**The 2019 Change:** You no longer file these with the County Clerk for incorporated entities; you file them centrally with the SOS. However, if the client is an *unincorporated* person (a sole proprietorship), you still file at the county level. Getting this distinction right is vital for maintaining the "corporate veil."

### 5. Expedited vs. Standard Processing
If you have a closing on Friday, you can't rely on standard processing. The Texas SOS offers an "Expedited" service for an additional fee (usually $25 per document).
