# Your Firm's Docketing System Might Be Quietly Hurting You

In the legal world, a missed deadline isn't just a mistake—it’s a catastrophe. According to malpractice insurance data, "calendar errors" and "missed deadlines" are consistently the top causes of claims against solo and small firms.

But here is the scary part: your docketing system might be "hurting" you even if you haven't missed a deadline yet. How? By creating a constant, low-level background radiation of **stress**. 

If you wake up at 3:00 AM wondering if you calculated the 30-day response window correctly under the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, your system is broken. Here is how to audit your firm's docketing and move from "Anxiety" to "Automation."

### 1. The "Single Source of Truth"
If you have some deadlines in your Outlook, some in a paper planner, and some on a whiteboard, you have no system at all. You need a "Single Source of Truth"—a centralized, digital calendar where *every* case milestone is recorded.

### 2. The "Three-Day Buffer" Rule
A deadline is not a "Goal." It’s a hard wall. We advocate for a "Three-Day Buffer" protocol. 
- **Internal Deadline:** 3 business days before the filing.
- **Review Deadline:** 1 business day before the filing.
- **Statutory Deadline:** The actual due date.
By aiming for the internal deadline, you give yourself breathing room for technical glitches, client delays, or emergencies.

### 3. Rule-Based Calculating (The Texas Advantage)
Texas deadlines are technical. Did you count from the date of *service* or the date of *filing*? Does the "Mailbox Rule" apply? 
We use rule-based docketing logic to ensure that every calculation is based on the current TRCP. We don't just "guess"; we verify the math against the rules every single time.

### 4. The "Second Pair of Eyes"
Even the best attorneys make arithmetic errors. A "Self-Docketing" attorney is a risk. You need a second set of eyes—usually a trusted paralegal—who verifies every entry on the master calendar. 

### 5. Automated Reminders (The Nag Factor)
A good system shouldn't just "be there"; it should "find you." You should receive automated reminders:
- **Two Weeks Out:** To begin the drafting.
- **One Week Out:** To finalize the production.
- **48 Hours Out:** To perform the final cite-check and e-file.
