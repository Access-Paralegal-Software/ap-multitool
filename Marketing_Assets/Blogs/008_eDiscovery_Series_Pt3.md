# eDiscovery for Solo & Small Firms (Pt. 3): Production, Presentation, and Defensibility

We’ve identified the data. We’ve collected it. We’ve reviewed it. Now comes the moment of truth: **Production**. 

In the Texas legal system, "Production" is more than just hitting "Send" on an email. It is the formal act of handing over evidence to opposing counsel. If you do this incorrectly, you risk waiving privilege or, worse, being sanctioned for a "non-responsive" production. Here is how to cross the finish line with confidence.

### Phase 6: Production (The Format Matters)
In Pt. 2, we talked about "Native" files. In the Production phase, you usually have to decide between **Native**, **Image (TIFF/PDF)**, or a **Hybrid** production. 
- **PDF Productions:** Most common for small firms. They are easy to read and Bates stamp.
- **Native Productions:** Required for things like Excel spreadsheets (where the formulas are the evidence).
- **Load Files:** For larger cases, you’ll need a "Load File" (.DAT or .OPT) that allows the other side to import your production into their software.

**The Access Paralegal Rule:** Always agree on a "Protocol for Production" with opposing counsel *before* you produce a single page. It prevents "Format Wars" later.

### Phase 7: Presentation & Trial Prep
Once the documents are produced, the real work of trial prep begins. 
- **Deposition Prep:** Linking your production sets to specific deposition outlines.
- **Exhibit Lists:** Selecting the "Hot" documents from your review and turning them into formal exhibits.
- **Demonstratives:** Creating visual aids (charts, timelines) based on the produced data.

The goal of eDiscovery isn't just to produce documents; it's to find the **story** within the data and present it persuasively to a judge or jury.

### Phase 8: Defensibility (The "Sleep at Night" Factor)
If opposing counsel challenges your production—claiming you missed a key email or withheld data—you must be able to defend your process. This is why we maintain an **Audit Trail** for every case.
- What were the search terms?
- What were the date ranges?
- Who performed the collection?
- How was the privilege review handled?

Defensibility means being able to stand in front of a judge and say, *"Your Honor, we followed a standardized, expert protocol. Here is the documentation to prove it."*

### Final Thoughts on the EDRM
eDiscovery for solos isn't about having the most expensive software. It’s about having a **reproducible, defensible process**. By following the EDRM framework, you protect your client, your firm, and your reputation.
