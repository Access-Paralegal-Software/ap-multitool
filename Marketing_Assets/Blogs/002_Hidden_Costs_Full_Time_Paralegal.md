# The Hidden Costs of Hiring a Full-Time Paralegal

When attorneys look at the cost of support, they usually start and end with the salary. 

*"I can hire a junior paralegal for $55k. That's about $26 an hour. Why would I pay a freelance paralegal a higher hourly rate?"*

It’s a fair question, but it misses the "Iceberg Effect." The salary is just the tip of the iceberg visible above the water. Beneath the surface are the hidden costs that can quietly sink a small firm's profitability.

### 1. The 1.4x Rule
Economists generally estimate that a full-time employee costs roughly **1.2 to 1.4 times** their base salary once you factor in the "extras." For a $55k paralegal, your actual cost is closer to $77k. 
- **Payroll Taxes:** Social Security, Medicare, and unemployment insurance.
- **Benefits:** Health, dental, and vision insurance premiums.
- **Retirement:** 401(k) matching or profit-sharing.

### 2. The Cost of "Empty Time"
When you hire a full-time employee, you are paying for their *presence*, not just their *productivity*. 
In a typical 8-hour day, how many hours are actually spent on billable or high-value work? Between lunch breaks, office socializing, and the inevitable "slow periods" between filings, you are likely paying for 10–15 hours a week of downtime. 

With a freelance paralegal, you pay for the **minute** the work starts until the minute it finishes. You pay for 100% productivity.

### 3. Software and Hardware
Every new hire needs a high-powered workstation, dual monitors (a must for litigation), and a suite of licenses. 
- **Westlaw/LexisNexis seats**
- **Adobe Acrobat Pro**
- **Clio/MyCase licenses**
- **Microsoft 365**
These "seat costs" can easily add $300–$500 per month per user. A freelance paralegal provides their own hardware and often their own specialized software, removing that burden from your balance sheet.

### 4. The Recruitment and Training Drain
The time you spend interviewing, onboarding, and training a new hire is time you aren't billing. If it takes 40 hours of your time to get a junior paralegal up to speed, and your billable rate is $350, that new hire just cost you **$14,000 in lost revenue** before they ever touched a file.

### The Bottom Line
For larger firms with a constant, high-volume caseload, full-time staff makes sense. But for the growth-minded solo or small firm, the "Hidden Costs" of hiring are often the biggest barrier to scaling. 

Freelance support isn't just a service—it's a financial strategy that keeps your firm lean, agile, and ready to compete with the big players.
