# Cite-Checking Like Your Reputation Depends on It

You’ve spent forty hours drafting the "perfect" Motion for Summary Judgment. The logic is sound, the facts are compelling, and the law is on your side. 

Then, the judge’s clerk opens the file. They click on your first major case citation and—*error*. The page number is wrong. They look at your second citation—*superseded by statute*. 

In an instant, your credibility has taken a hit. If the attorney can't be bothered to check their page numbers, can the judge trust their interpretation of the law?

Cite-checking isn't just "formatting." It is the foundation of legal credibility. At Access Paralegal Services, we treat every citation like the entire case depends on it. Here is the protocol we use to ensure your motions are bulletproof.

### 1. The "Double-Blind" Verification
We don't just look at the citation in your draft. We pull the original opinion from Westlaw or LexisNexis every single time. We verify the party names, the volume, the reporter, and the starting page number. Then, we verify the **pin cite**. If you’re quoting a specific sentence, we make sure that sentence exists on that exact page.

### 2. The Green Flag is Not Enough
Many attorneys rely on the "Green Flag" or "Positive Treatment" icon in their research software. That is a dangerous shortcut. A case can have a green flag and still be irrelevant to your specific point, or it may have been subtly narrowed by a more recent appellate decision. We read the treatment notes to ensure the law is still "good" for the specific proposition you’re citing.

### 3. The Bluebook (or Greenbook) Standard
Whether you’re in Federal Court following the Bluebook or in a Texas State Court adhering to the *Texas Rules of Form* (the "Greenbook"), consistency is key. We check every italicized comma and every parenthetical. Why? Because a messy brief suggests a messy mind. A clean, perfectly formatted brief signals to the court that this attorney is precise, disciplined, and authoritative.

### 4. TOC and TOA: The Final Polish
The Table of Contents and Table of Authorities are the "Map" of your brief. If your TOA says a case is on page 14, but it’s actually on page 15, the clerk is going to be frustrated. We automate the generation but manually verify every entry to ensure the "Map" is 100% accurate.

### The Value of the Second Set of Eyes
When you’ve been looking at a brief for three days straight, your brain starts to "autocorrect" errors. You see what you *expect* to see, not what is actually on the page. 

By bringing in a freelance paralegal for a final cite-check and Bluebook scrub, you’re getting a fresh, expert perspective that catches the "invisible" errors before the court does.
