# Why More Texas Solo Attorneys Are Turning to Freelance Paralegals

If you’re running a solo practice in Texas, you already know the "Solo Trap." You have enough work to keep you busy 60 hours a week, but not quite enough consistent revenue to justify a $65k/year salary plus benefits for a full-time paralegal. 

So you stay stuck. You spend your nights cite-checking motions and your weekends organizing production sets instead of doing the high-level strategy and client development that actually grows your firm.

Lately, I’ve seen a massive shift in how my clients—solo and small-firm attorneys from Houston to El Paso—are solving this. They aren't hiring; they're *outsourcing*. Here is why the freelance paralegal model is winning in the Texas legal market right now.

### 1. Overhead is the Enemy of the Solo
Hiring a full-time employee isn't just about the salary. It's the payroll taxes, the health insurance, the 401(k) matching, and the literal desk space. In a post-2020 world, many Texas solos have moved to lean, virtual, or hybrid models. Why pay for a 40-hour-a-week seat when you only need 10 hours of high-level litigation support?

### 2. Expertise on Demand
When you hire a generalist paralegal, they might be "okay" at everything. When you bring in a freelance specialist, you’re getting 20+ years of experience in specific niches—like EDRM-compliant eDiscovery or complex Secretary of State filings—exactly when the case demands it. You get AmLaw 200 quality without the AmLaw price tag.

### 3. The "Texas-Wide" Advantage
Texas is huge. Between the Harris County e-filing quirks and the specific local rules in North Texas federal courts, having a paralegal who knows the *entire* landscape is vital. Freelance paralegals are built for remote work, meaning you can have the best support in the state regardless of where your office is located.

### 4. Reclaiming Your Time
The most successful solos I work with have one thing in common: they know their hourly rate is too high to be spent on administrative tasks. By handing off the "heavy lifting" of document management and initial drafting to a trusted freelance partner, they're finally able to focus on the work only *they* can do.
