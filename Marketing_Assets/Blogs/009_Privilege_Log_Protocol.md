# How to Prepare a Privilege Log That Holds Up

In the world of Texas discovery, a privilege log is one of the most common flashpoints for litigation. 

You’ve withheld 500 documents on the basis of attorney-client privilege. Opposing counsel, naturally, doesn't believe you. They demand a log. If that log is vague, incomplete, or formatted incorrectly, you are handing them a silver platter for a **Motion to Compel**.

Under **Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 193.3**, you have a specific obligation to describe the withheld information so that the other party can assess the applicability of the privilege. Here is the Access Paralegal protocol for drafting a log that holds up under scrutiny.

### 1. The Description is Key
The most common mistake is being too vague. 
- **Wrong:** "Email regarding case strategy."
- **Right:** "Email from lead attorney to client regarding initial assessment of liability and draft pleading strategy."

You must provide enough detail to show that the communication was (a) between an attorney and client, and (b) made for the purpose of facilitating the rendition of legal services. 

### 2. Identifying the "Actors"
A good privilege log should include a "Key" or a list of the individuals involved. You need to clearly identify who is the attorney, who is the client representative, and who is a third-party (like a consultant or an expert) who falls under the "Work Product" or "Consulting Expert" umbrella. If a document includes a third party without a clear privilege hook, you’ve likely waived the privilege.

### 3. Metadata Accuracy
A privilege log isn't just a Word doc; it's a data set. Every entry should include:
- **Date**
- **Author**
- **Recipients (and CC/BCC)**
- **Document Type** (Email, Memo, Draft)
- **The specific privilege asserted** (Attorney-Client, Work Product, etc.)

If your log’s dates don't match the metadata of the original files, opposing counsel will use that inconsistency to challenge the entire log's credibility.

### 4. The "Partial Production" (Redactions)
Sometimes, a document is only partially privileged. In those cases, you produce the document with redactions and include it on the log. The description should specify exactly what was redacted (e.g., "Redacted portion contains legal advice regarding potential settlement figures").
