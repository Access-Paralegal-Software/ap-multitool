# Legal Project Management for Lawyers Who Hate Project Management

If the phrase "Legal Project Management" (LPM) makes you want to close your laptop and take a nap, you aren't alone. 

Most attorneys went to law school to practice law, not to build Gantt charts or manage "agile sprints." But as your firm grows from 5 cases to 50, the lack of a system starts to hurt. Deadlines get missed, client communication slips, and your "to-do list" becomes a source of existential dread.

Here is the good news: **You don't have to be a Project Manager.** You just need to have a project management *mindset*—and a paralegal who can execute it.

### 1. The "Phase-Based" Approach
Litigation is a project. It has a beginning (Petitions/Answers), a middle (Discovery/Motions), and an end (Trial/Settlement). 
LPM simply means looking at each case as a series of milestones. Instead of worrying about "The Smith Case," we focus on "Phase 2: Discovery Completion." This allows you to allocate your energy and your budget more effectively.

### 2. Standardization is Your Friend
The #1 killer of productivity in a solo firm is "Reinventing the Wheel." 
Every time you open a new case, you should have a standardized **Master Case Tree** and a set of **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)**.
- How do we handle intake?
- Where do we store the client's medical records?
- What is the naming convention for our pleadings?
By standardizing these "low-value" tasks, you free up your brain for "high-value" legal analysis.

### 3. The Power of the Dashboard
You shouldn't have to open a case file to know its status. A simple dashboard (whether it’s in Clio, MyCase, or a shared spreadsheet) should tell you:
- What is the next major deadline?
- Are we waiting on a response from opposing counsel?
- Is the client up-to-date on their billing?
