# How to Force a Priority Document to the Top of a Large Queue

*Pillar: Workflow & How-To | Target keyword: reorder PDF document queue priority position paralegal*

---

There's a specific situation every paralegal runs into: you have a large queue of documents loaded and ordered, and then you need to move one specific document — a cover sheet, a certification, a key exhibit — to a precise position, usually near the top.

If you're using drag-and-drop, moving a document from position 43 to position 1 means clicking, holding, and dragging through 42 intermediate positions. In a long list, this is slow, imprecise, and frustrating.

There's a faster way.

## Direct Position Entry: The Two-Second Move

APMultitool's queue includes a position column — the `#` column — that shows the current position of each document in the merge order. This column is also an input field.

To move any document to any position:

1. Double-click the `#` field on the document you want to move
2. Type the position number you want (e.g., `1` to move to the top)
3. Press Enter

The document immediately jumps to that position. Every other document in the queue automatically shifts to accommodate — no other positions need to be manually adjusted.

For a queue of 50 documents, moving the priority document from position 50 to position 1 takes exactly two seconds.

## When to Use This Instead of Drag-and-Drop

Drag-and-drop is the right tool for coarse ordering — getting broad groups of documents into the right general sequence when you're doing initial organization. It's intuitive and fast for short moves.

Direct position entry is the right tool for:

**Long-distance moves.** Any time a document needs to travel more than 5–6 positions, typing the target position is faster than dragging.

**Precision requirements.** When the document needs to land at exactly position 3 (not approximately third), position entry is more reliable than drag-and-drop.

**Final adjustments before merge.** After you've done the coarse ordering, if you discover a misplaced document during your queue review, position entry lets you fix it instantly without disturbing the surrounding order.

**Attorney-directed changes.** When the attorney reviews the queue and says "move that one to the top," direct position entry executes that instruction in two seconds with no error margin.

## The Most Common Use Case: Cover Sheets and Certification Pages

Many court filings require a cover sheet or certification page as the first exhibit in a packet. These documents often don't load first in the queue — they may have been added to the folder after the other exhibits, or they may be named in a way that puts them alphabetically at the end of the load order.

Rather than renaming files or reorganizing your folder before loading, load everything and then use position entry to place the cover sheet at position 1. The rest of the queue remains exactly as it was.

This is also useful for:
- An exhibit that needs to immediately follow the cover sheet (position 2, regardless of its filename)
- A supplemental exhibit that was added late and needs to go at the beginning of a specific section
- Any document where the attorney has given you a specific position requirement

## Combining Both Methods

The most efficient queue management workflow uses both methods together:

1. Load all documents
2. Use drag-and-drop for initial broad ordering — getting exhibits into the right general groups
3. Review the queue from top to bottom against your exhibit list
4. Use direct position entry to correct any specific placements that need adjustment
5. Do a final top-to-bottom review
6. Merge

The combination is faster than either method alone, because each tool is being used for what it's best at.
