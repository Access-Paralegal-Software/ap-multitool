# What Happens to Client Files When You Use a Free Online PDF Tool?

*Pillar: Security & Compliance | Target keyword: free online PDF tool legal documents risk*

---

Free online PDF tools are everywhere. Merge PDFs, split them, compress them, convert them — all for nothing, right in your browser, no software to install. For a paralegal under deadline pressure who just needs to combine six documents quickly, the appeal is obvious.

But nothing is actually free. And when the product is free, it's worth asking what the business model actually is.

## Where the Revenue Comes From

Free PDF tools are businesses. They pay for servers, engineers, and infrastructure. That cost gets covered somehow — usually through advertising, premium upsells, or data.

The data angle is the one that concerns legal professionals.

Some free tools retain uploaded files for a period of time — to improve their processing algorithms, for customer service purposes, or simply because their retention policy hasn't been written tightly. Some store metadata about the files even after deletion. Some process documents through pipelines shared with third-party services.

This isn't speculation. It's disclosed in terms of service that almost no one reads.

## What the Terms of Service Actually Say

Pull up the terms of service for any popular free PDF tool and look for the sections on data usage, file storage, and third-party sharing. You'll typically find language like:

- Files are stored for [X hours/days] after processing
- Uploaded content may be reviewed to improve service quality
- We may share information with service providers who assist in operating the platform

Each of those phrases represents a moment where your client's confidential legal documents are somewhere other than your computer.

Your firm's confidentiality obligations don't have a carve-out for "I didn't read the terms of service."

## The Scenarios That Actually Happen

Breaches don't always look dramatic. Sometimes they look like this:

A paralegal at a small firm routinely uses a free online merger to combine discovery documents. The tool stores files for 24 hours. The tool gets breached. A dataset of uploaded files — including documents with client names, case numbers, and financial records — is exposed. The firm learns about it from the news.

Or: A paralegal uses a browser-based PDF compressor on their work computer. The tool logs document metadata and the IP address. The client is in litigation against a company that has relationships with the tool's parent company. The metadata is theoretically accessible.

These aren't common events. But they're not impossible ones, either.

## What You Can Actually Control

You can't audit every cloud service your firm uses. But you can control which tools touch client documents during production.

The alternative is simple: software that runs on your machine, processes documents on your machine, and doesn't need the internet to do its job. When a document never leaves your computer, none of the scenarios above are possible.

That's not a technical distinction. It's a professional one.

## Before You Use a Free Tool on Client Files

Ask three questions:
1. Does this tool upload my file to a server?
2. How long does it retain uploaded files?
3. Has anyone at this firm reviewed the terms of service?

If you don't know the answers, you don't actually know what's happening to your client's documents.
