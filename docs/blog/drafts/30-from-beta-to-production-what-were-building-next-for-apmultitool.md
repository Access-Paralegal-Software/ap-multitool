# From Beta to Production: What We're Building Next for APMultitool

*Pillar: Product Deep-Dive | Target keyword: APMultitool roadmap future features legal document software*

---

APMultitool launched its first Windows beta with a focused set of capabilities: PDF merging, visual queue ordering, permanent Bates stamping with registry tracking, audit log generation, and a Case Vault for session context. These were the core functions that legal support professionals needed, built on an offline-first foundation that keeps client documents on the machine where they belong.

Beta is the beginning of the conversation. The features in v1.0.0-beta1 were shaped by our understanding of paralegal document production workflows. The features that come next will be shaped by what actual users tell us matters in practice.

This post is about where we're headed and why.

## What Beta Is For

A beta release is not a finished product with a few rough edges. It's a complete, usable version of the core workflow — stable enough to do real work, early enough to change direction based on what we learn.

For APMultitool beta, "doing real work" means assembling court-ready exhibit packets with correct Bates numbering and complete audit trails. If you can do that reliably with v1.0.0-beta1, the beta is succeeding at its purpose.

The feedback we're most interested in during this phase: where does the workflow break down? What does the tool ask you to do manually that it should be able to do for you? What features are you missing that would make document production meaningfully better?

Send that feedback to info@accessparalegalservices.com. It directly shapes the roadmap.

## Near-Term: Polish and Reliability

The first priority after beta feedback is stability and polish — making the existing workflow more robust and handling edge cases that real-world usage surfaces.

Areas we're watching:
- **Large document set performance.** Threading keeps the UI responsive, but very large sets (200+ documents) may reveal optimization opportunities in the processing pipeline.
- **Edge case PDF handling.** Most PDFs process cleanly. Some PDFs — scanned documents, encrypted files, PDFs generated by unusual software — may require additional handling.
- **Error messaging.** When something goes wrong, the message should tell you what happened and what to do about it, not just that an error occurred.

These improvements happen based on what users actually encounter, which is why the support bundle and feedback channel exist.

## Medium-Term: Workflow Expansion

Based on the pattern of requests from legal support professionals we've talked with during development, here are the areas most likely to be developed next:

**Email attachment harvesting.** Many paralegals spend significant time extracting PDF attachments from email threads — downloading each one individually, organizing them by folder, then loading them into a production tool. An integrated workflow that identifies and extracts PDF attachments from a mail export or folder into the production queue would eliminate a multi-step manual process.

**Production sets and batch tracking.** Currently, each merge session stands alone. Grouping multiple merge sessions into a named production set — "Discovery Round 2, March 15, 2026" — would make it easier to track and reference complete productions that span multiple sessions.

**Document preview in the queue.** Seeing a thumbnail or first-page preview of each document in the queue without opening it would help verify that the right documents are loaded in the right positions, especially in large queues with similarly-named files.

**Exhibit label overlays.** Some productions require exhibit labels or separator sheets — "Exhibit A," "Exhibit B" — to be inserted between documents. Generating these automatically and inserting them at the correct queue positions would save significant manual effort.

## Longer-Term: Platform Expansion

Windows first. That's where the majority of paralegal workstations are, and that's where the beta is focused.

Mac and Linux support are on the roadmap. The design intentionally avoids Windows-specific dependencies that would complicate cross-platform porting. The question is sequencing: we want to do Windows right before expanding to other platforms.

## What Won't Change

The core design commitments of APMultitool are not subject to revision based on product roadmap pressure:

**Offline-first.** Client documents will always be processed locally. Adding cloud features does not mean adding cloud document processing. If we ever build cloud capabilities, they will be explicitly scoped, explicitly optional, and will not touch document content.

**No surveillance.** No telemetry that reports on your document production activity. No analytics that capture case information or document metadata. If the application needs to communicate with our servers for any reason, you'll know what it's sending and why.

**Affordable for small firms.** The paralegal at a solo practice and the paralegal at a 10-attorney firm should be able to afford the same quality of document production tool. Pricing will reflect that.

## How to Be Part of What Gets Built

Use the beta. Submit feedback when something doesn't work the way you expect. Tell us what's missing from your production workflow. The product roadmap is not a fixed document — it's shaped by the people who are actually using the tool.

The beta testers who report bugs, suggest features, and describe their workflows are the ones who most directly influence what gets built. That's not a figure of speech — it's how the roadmap actually works at this stage.
